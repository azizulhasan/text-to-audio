# Why this approach (per email type)

| Element | YouTuber emails | Editorial pitches |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | One specific reference to their work | Direct pitch — no false familiarity |
| Hook | Free lifetime license is the lure | A story angle they don't already have |
| Proof | 315K downloads + 4.8 stars | Specific narrative (wp.org closure) they can't get from elsewhere |
| CTA | Low-friction: "If interested, I'll send the license" | Low-friction: "Open to an interview" or "I can have a draft in 7 days" |
| Honesty marker | "No script, no editorial control" | "Not pitching a product" |

The lifetime license offer matters: most WP YouTubers get pitched a 1-year license + script. A lifetime Enterprise license + free editorial control is a meaningfully better offer. Cost to AtlasVoice = zero cash, ~$699 in foregone retail per recipient (negligible because no YouTuber would have bought it anyway).

The closure story for editorial: this is the only thing AtlasVoice has right now that other WordPress plugin makers DON'T have. It's a real story. Pitching it lands because it's not a product placement — it's reporting.
